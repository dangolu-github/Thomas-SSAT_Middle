Poetry Reading 1 · The Glasshouse in Rain

1All afternoon the rain worked at the glass,2and water stitched the garden paths together.3Inside, I counted labels in the pots,4each one a name for something not yet seen.5The seed packet had promised early flowers;6I read the words again beside the door.7One brittle leaf lay curled upon the bench,8and every empty pot resembled failure.

9Then, near the pane the weak sun touched at noon,10I saw the earth begin to yield a little.11Two narrow leaves stood clear of yesterday,12small green commas in an unfinished page.13I moved the pots to catch that patch of light,14but left a space between their shallow trays.15The rain still tapped against the glass above;16it had not heard that anything had changed.

17I cannot promise flowers before Friday,18or say that every buried seed will rise.19The labels keep their places in the shadows;20the smallest pots still hold their private weather.21Yet when I turn the key and cross the garden,22I do not count the empty pots again.23I think instead of how two leaves stood upright,24and how the earth had loosened at their edges.25The rain follows me softly to the gate.26I carry home a tentative belief27that work may happen where I cannot see it,28and that tomorrow is a place to look.

Poetry Reading 2 · The Mended Coat

1My mother laid the coat across her knees,2its elbow worn as thin as winter light.3I watched her choose a square of darker cloth4and wished that she would find a closer match.5At school, I thought, the patch would speak before me;6I saw the turning heads, the questions asked.7The room was warm. I kept my reasons folded8and said the sleeve was hardly worth repair.

9She turned the coat and showed its inner seam,10where someone else had left a line of blue.11"Your brother caught it climbing by the mill."12Her needle paused, then travelled on again.13I knew the mill, the fence, the crooked willow;14I had not known the coat had been there too.15The stitches made a narrow road between16the faded sleeve and cloth that still was strong.

17Next morning I put on the mended coat.18The patch was plain; no careful turn could hide it.19I brushed a thread away and took my books,20then met my mother waiting by the door.21I did not ask her if the colours matched.22Outside, the air pressed cold against my face.23I put my elbow to the wooden gate24and felt the strengthened cloth hold firm beneath it.

Reading Questions

Use the poems as evidence. Questions 1–5 refer to The Glasshouse in Rain; questions 6–10 refer to The Mended Coat.
